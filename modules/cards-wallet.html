Module 06

Cards Wallet & Sharing

In progress

Every card you make lives here — spin through them on the pedestal, flip one over, and its back carries a real QR code that opens fullscreen for scanning across a table. Sharing goes well past the code: send the link through any app, copy it, pass a contact file, duplicate a design, or write your card onto a physical NFC tag.

01

Flip through your cards

Built · live on the cloud

Swipe through every card you own, flip the one in focus, and show its QR big enough to scan from across a table.

02

Share your card

Built · every route works

Every box on this sheet works today. The tag writer really writes, with a 20-second window and an honest outcome either way; the QR goes out as a branded picture, the card as a contact file the address book imports directly, and the app row reaches WhatsApp, LinkedIn, X, Instagram, Facebook, Telegram, Messenger, email and SMS — or hands off to the phone's own sheet for anything else installed. One honest exception: the wallet-pass button opens a "not configured yet" page until the issuer accounts exist — a business registration, tracked under Things to do.

03

Manage the wallet

Built · saves to the cloud

Any number of cards can be active at once — pausing one keeps its data but takes it out of circulation, and a paused card politely refuses to share.

04

After the card leaves your phone

Built · live today

What happens out in the world now comes back to you. Every open of your public card page is counted as a card view. If the person shares their own details back, the introduction waits for your approval — it arrives as a notification and, since today, also sits on Home until you deal with it. Once approved, the new contact remembers it arrived by share-back, and Home's network card shows the running numbers.

A

How the wallet is wired

Live today

Your cards live in the cloud and come back on any phone you sign in on — the link, the QR and the tag writer all read from the same saved card, and pictures upload to cloud storage alongside it, so a reinstall loses nothing. New since today: every open of your public page and every share-back is counted, and Home reads those numbers.

A card's two lifecycles

Built

Two independent lifecycles: how the focused card is displayed on screen, and whether it circulates out in the world.

T

What powers this module

Feature by feature — what each moving part actually runs on.

  • Wallet & card flipReanimated circular carousel — cards loop around the pedestal · tap-to-flip card backs on the same animation stack
  • QR codereact-native-qrcode-svg (pure JS) rendering the card's public link — on the card back and blown up fullscreen for scanning
  • Share sheet@gorhom/bottom-sheet modal · the phone's own share sheet for text, so every destination behaves the way it normally does — messaging apps open their composer, Notes makes a note, browsers open the link, Copy copies · react-native-share for pictures and files, because Android's built-in sharing cannot attach either
  • The app rowNine apps addressed directly, then a hand-off to the phone's own sheet. Each is reached by its published web link rather than a private app scheme, so a tap still works on a phone without the app installed. Instagram, Facebook and Messenger cannot legally be handed text, so those copy the link and open the app
  • Seeing other appsAndroid 11 hides installed apps from us unless we declare what we want to reach. That declaration was missing, which is why sharing to a specific app quietly did nothing on newer phones — the app was there, the system simply would not admit it. Now declared for text, pictures, contact files, browsers and the nine named apps
  • Contact filevCard 3.0 built in-app, offered first as the type Android's address book, Gmail and WhatsApp actually filter on — the newer standard name is tried second, and the card falls back to plain text so the button always does something
  • NFC tag writingreact-native-nfc-manager — a real NDEF URI write, 20-second tag window, graceful timeout and off-state detection
  • Card storageLive Supabase cards table (PostgreSQL) with owner-scoped access rules — create, edit, duplicate, pause and delete all save to your account today, and only you can read your own cards
  • Card picturesLive in Cloudflare R2 object storage — photos, logos and covers upload for real and survive a reinstall. The app never holds the storage keys; a server function signs short-lived upload and download links scoped to the owner's own folder
  • Where contacts came fromLive — migration 0010 applied and verified 1 Aug. Each contact records its own route in — imported, QR scanned, card photographed, typed in, or shared back — counted per account in PostgreSQL, and Home's network card reads these numbers today alongside card views and share-backs. The same counts are readable by the CRM and OKR products later, rather than a figure only this app understands. Applying it also fixed a quiet blocker: the database had been refusing contacts that recorded a scan route, so scanned contacts — QR and card photo — save again
  • Save reliabilityA native crash when saving a card — a text field still focused while the screen was torn down — was found and fixed, and the same guard now covers every form screen
P

Things to do

The big items that sat here have shipped: the public card page is live, the wallet-pass flow is built into the app, a card can be pinned to the home screen, and as of today every contact records how it was acquired — numbers Home now shows. What is left is one business step and the two iPhone checks, which are listed but not counted.

Integrations 3 items

  • Wallet issuer accountsThe in-app flow is done and the pass endpoint is live. What remains is a business registration, not engineering: Google states its Wallet API is unavailable to India-registered businesses, and Apple Wallet needs a developer account and a signing certificate. Until those exist the button opens an honest "not configured yet" page rather than failing silently
  • NFC tag writing on iPhoneiPhone · not countedNeeds the paid Apple developer membership — the free account cannot grant the NFC capability. Android writes real tags today; iPhone shows a graceful message until then
  • The iPhone home-screen widgetiPhone · not countedAndroid's home-screen widget ships and shows the code with no signal needed. The iPhone equivalent is written but needs its own build target plus the paid Apple membership for the data sharing it relies on
