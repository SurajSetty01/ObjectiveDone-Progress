Module 11

Meeting Recorder

In progress

Recording now has a room of its own — opened from the mic icon on Home or the Record quick-action card, exactly as you directed, rather than being tucked inside a contact. One tab records: choose meeting or voice note, watch the live waveform, pause and resume at will, and if you reach the time limit the take is saved, never thrown away. The other tab is the archive of every recording made anywhere in the app — meetings, voice notes and the memos attached to contacts, together in one searchable, sortable list, each one a tap from playing, renaming or being turned into words.

01

Press record → filed and kept

Built

From either doorway on Home to a titled recording safely in your account. Say whether it is a meeting or a voice note, record with a live waveform, pause and resume as often as you like — and if the time limit arrives before you stop, the take is saved automatically, not discarded. Review it, keep or change the suggested dated title, and Save uploads it and files it under History.

02

One archive of everything you have recorded

Built

History is not just this screen's recordings — it gathers every recording from anywhere in the app, including the voice memos kept on contacts, under headers that say where each came from. Search covers titles, transcripts and people; filter by origin; sort newest, oldest or longest. Every row plays, renames inline, transcribes behind the same paid gate as contact memos, or deletes — and a row that belongs to a contact opens that contact.

A

Where recordings live

Live on the cloud

Saving a take uploads the audio itself to private cloud file storage, and a recordings table lists it against your account — only your account can ever read your recordings. The memos kept on contacts sit in the same ledger, which is how History can show one merged list. Turning speech into words runs on the same engine that transcribes contact memos, so a standalone recording and a contact's memo become text the exact same way.

B

Fair limits — and why they exist

Built

Keeping audio in the cloud is a real, per-minute cost that never stops while a file is stored — so the free tier carries sensible ceilings, and Premium lifts them. Nothing about the limits ever costs you a recording: reaching a cap saves the take, it never discards it.

  • Free · one takeUp to 10 minutes per recording. Reaching the limit auto-stops and saves — the take goes to review and History like any other, nothing is lost
  • Free · what is keptUp to 60 minutes of stored audio in total across all recordings. Deleting a recording frees its minutes straight away, so the allowance is yours to manage
  • PremiumUp to 2 hours per take and no cap on what is kept
  • Why the ceilingsStored audio bills the club for every minute it sits in the cloud, month after month. The free limits keep that cost honest while still covering everyday use; heavy recorders carry their own weight on Premium
T

What powers this module

Feature by feature — what each moving part actually runs on. Notably: no new vendors anywhere in this module.

  • Capturing soundThe phone's own microphone, driven by the recorder engine the app already uses for contact voice memos — the live waveform, pause/resume and the auto-stop timer all run on the phone itself
  • Where the audio goesPrivate cloud file storage, the same service that already holds the app's pictures · each file belongs to one account and is reachable by no one else
  • The recordings ledgerA cloud table listing every take — its title, origin, length and transcript — with owner-only access, so History can merge meetings, voice notes and contact memos into one list without ever crossing accounts
  • Turning speech into wordsThe shared transcription model — the very one behind contact memos — behind the same paid gate, so a transcript reads and costs the same wherever the recording was made
  • New vendorsNone. Every part of this module runs on services the app already uses and pays for
