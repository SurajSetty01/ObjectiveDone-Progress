How one subscription pays for four different AI vendors.

This is the architecture as implemented, not as proposed. It exists because the question "if a member pays ₹400, how does that reach Anthropic and Cloudflare?" has a specific answer, and the answer determines how billing, metering and provider choice are built. Read it before changing any of the three.

Overview Features & tools What each use costs Free & Premium AI architecture

The one idea everything else follows from

The providers are invisible to the subscription. We do not resell tokens. We sell outcomes — a card read, a question answered, a minute transcribed — and the provider mix behind each outcome is an internal cost decision that can change in a week without touching billing, the app, or anything the member sees. Notion AI, Grammarly, Superhuman and Raycast are all built this way, and it is the only shape that survives a provider price change.
Nobody's ₹400 is split into envelopes. We hold one account with each vendor and pay them from it. No member gets their own API key and no key is ever put on a phone — anything shipped inside an app can be extracted from it, and an extracted key is billed to whoever owns it at whatever rate the finder likes. Pooling is also the entire margin: most members will not use their allowance, a few will use a lot, and a price is set against the average. Ten separate ₹40 wallets cannot average out; one pool does.

The gate sits after everything free and immediately before the one line that costs money. Auth, empty input and oversize input all refuse without spending a use — so a malformed request never burns a member's allowance. And a refusal is not a broken feature: card reading still returns the on-device extraction, so the member gets a filled-in form and an upsell rather than an error.

Metering: two numbers, on purpose

What the member sees

Uses
business units, never tokens

"5 card reads a day." A member cannot reason about tokens, and exposing them invites gaming. Free limits live in feature_limits; the daily counter lives in feature_usage.

What we see

Tokens
per call, per provider, per model

The real cost of every request. This is how margin gets measured and how abuse is spotted. It is related to the member-facing number only at design time, when allowances are set so worst-case cost stays well under the price.

Why the recording and photo caps are enforced in the app while the AI caps are enforced on the server. They are different risks. Spending our money at a vendor must be gated where the member cannot reach it. A recording length and a photo picker cost us nothing until the bytes are uploaded — and the upload path is itself authenticated and keyed to the member. Client-side is the right place for a limit whose only job is to shape behaviour; server-side is the only place for a limit whose job is to protect a bill.

Changing a model

what it takes today, and what it should take
Every model id reads from an environment variable — WORKERS_AI_MODEL, WORKERS_AI_STT_MODEL — so switching is a dashboard change and a function restart, not a redeploy and never an app release. That is also the rollback: setting WORKERS_AI_MODEL back to @cf/meta/llama-3.3-70b-instruct-fp8-fast undoes the Qwen3 move entirely.

The next step, not yet built: a feature_models table holding (feature, priority, provider, model, unit cost) so routing is data rather than configuration, and a provider can be added without editing a function at all. That is the point at which "the providers are invisible to the subscription" becomes literally true rather than merely true in effect.

Open questions, stated rather than buried

• A very heavy concierge user is unprofitable. 750 questions a month costs ₹285 against a ₹400 price. feature_usage already records who they are; decide between a fair-use cap and a tier above before it matters.
• The concierge cannot answer aggregate questions. It is retrieval-based — it sees a shortlist of contacts, never a COUNT — so "how many contacts have a +91 number" is unanswerable and it will guess rather than decline. Fix is a precomputed stats block in the system prompt, then tool-calling for arbitrary aggregates. Not yet built.
• Mapbox's Indian address data is the known weak spot. Google Places or Ola Maps is the upgrade at ₹0.27 a request.
• Payments are not wired. Plans are granted by hand until Razorpay and its webhook exist; see docs/payments-integration.md.
