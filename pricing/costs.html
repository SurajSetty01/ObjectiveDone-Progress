The single biggest lever on the AI side: the concierge model. Haiku 4.5 answers a question for ₹0.31; Sonnet 5 costs ₹0.63 today and ₹0.94 from 1 September 2026 — triple. The plan maths on the next page assumes Haiku for everyday questions, which is the right default for "who did I meet at that event" lookups. Keep Sonnet for genuinely hard reasoning only.
Three things a vendor page would not confirm, stated plainly:
• The classic Whisper model no longer appears on OpenAI's price list at all. Its last sourceable rate is $0.006/min from third parties — so the plans use the officially-priced gpt-4o-mini-transcribe instead.
• Deepgram publishes $0.0048 and $0.0077 per minute but its table's column order for streaming versus pre-recorded is ambiguous. Budget the higher figure.
• logo.dev's $33.33 tier appears to be an annual-billing rate; month-to-month is not shown publicly. The free tier covers our volume for a long time regardless.

The one infrastructure decision that matters: at 5,000 members, 86% of the bill is the charge to send files back to phones — not to store them. Cloudflare R2 charges nothing for that, which turns ₹47,735 a month into ₹6,193. It is far easier to put recordings and photos there from the start than to move them later, so this should be settled before the app has real members. (Amazon S3's Mumbai region is worse than staying put — ₹56,286 — so it is not the alternative it looks like.)
Also worth knowing: Supabase ships with a spend cap switched on, which blocks overage instead of billing it. Left alone, the numbers above turn into the service quietly degrading rather than a larger invoice — so it needs to be turned off deliberately, with a billing alarm in its place. And every rupee figure here moves with the exchange rate; only the MSG91 and WhatsApp prices are natively in rupees.
