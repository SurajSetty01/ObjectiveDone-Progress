💬 One concierge question

₹0.38
Claude Haiku 4.5 with prompt caching — as shipped

2,000 cached in × $0.10/M = $0.00020
2,000 fresh in × $1.00/M = $0.00200
350 out × $5.00/M = $0.00175
= $0.00395 → ₹0.38

📇 One paper card read

₹0.08
on-device OCR + Llama 4 Scout reading the card image

OCR is free — ML Kit / Apple Vision, on the phone.
Measured on a real scan, 7 Aug 2026:
3,161 in + 82 out = 83.9 neurons
× $0.011 / 1,000 neurons = $0.00092 → ₹0.08 (₹77 per 1,000)
Was ₹0.01 on text-only Qwen3-30B (7.2 neurons) — this is 11.6× that. The image is ~2,000 of the 3,161 input tokens and is exactly what the increase buys: sending the card itself fixed the address, the name-with-initials and the phone/website fields that the text-only pipeline got wrong on every test card (ADR-041). Still a rounding error next to the ₹0.38 a Concierge question costs, and the 10,000 free neurons/day cover ~119 scans before anything is billed. The dial, if it ever matters: the image is downscaled to 1400px before sending, and input tokens fall roughly with the pixel count.

The concierge is the whole cost story, and it is the one place we did not take the cheapest option. At ₹0.38 a question it is 5× a card read and 8× a minute of transcription. Qwen3-30B would answer for ₹0.03 and Gemini Flash-Lite for ₹0.05 — but the concierge is the only feature where the model's tone is the product, and a card parser that is slightly worse produces a wrong phone number you can fix, while a concierge that is slightly worse just feels cheap. Prompt caching was taken instead: ~31% off, no quality change at all. If cost ever forces the issue, Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite is the first thing to try, and the swap is a config change rather than a rewrite.
Where this could still bite, stated plainly:
• A very heavy concierge user is unprofitable at ₹400. 750 questions in a month costs ₹285 against a ₹400 price. Two hundred questions — a genuinely heavy month — costs ₹76 and is comfortable. If the top 1% start living in the chat, cap it or price a tier above it; `feature_usage` already records exactly who they are.
• Qwen3 is a hybrid reasoning model. It narrates inside <think> tags unless told not to, and thinking tokens are billed as output. Both prompts send /no_think and the JSON parser strips any that survives — but a future model swap must re-check this, or output cost can quietly multiply.
• Mapbox's Indian address data is the known weak spot. It is free and it is fine for POIs; house-number lookups in Bengaluru are not its strength. Google Places or Ola Maps is the upgrade, at ₹0.27 a request.

The one infrastructure decision that matters: at 5,000 members, 86% of the bill is the charge to send files back to phones — not to store them. Cloudflare R2 charges nothing for that, which turns ₹47,735 a month into ₹6,193. It is far easier to put recordings and photos there from the start than to move them later, so this should be settled before the app has real members. (Amazon S3's Mumbai region is worse than staying put — ₹56,286 — so it is not the alternative it looks like.)
Also worth knowing: Supabase ships with a spend cap switched on, which blocks overage instead of billing it. Left alone, the numbers above turn into the service quietly degrading rather than a larger invoice — so it needs to be turned off deliberately, with a billing alarm in its place. And every rupee figure here moves with the exchange rate; only the MSG91 and WhatsApp prices are natively in rupees.
